A grammar $G=(V,T,S,P)$ is called **context-free** if all production rules are of the form $$A\to u$$ where $A\in V$ and $u\in (V\cup T)^{*}$. That is, the left-hand side of all rules is a single variable. A language $L$ is **context-free** if there is a context-free grammar with $L=L(G)$.

> [!question]
> Show that the following language is context-free: $$\{a^{n}b^{m}c^{2n} \,|\, n\geq 0, m \geq 0\}$$ 
> > [!tip]- Solution 
> > We give a context-free grammar for the language
> > $$\begin{align*}
> > S &\to aScc \, | \, T\\
> > T &\to bT \, | \, \lambda
> > \end{align*}$$
> > The start variable is $S$.

> [!question]
> Show that the following language is context-free: $$L = \Sigma^{*}\setminus \{ww \, | \, w \in \Sigma^{*}\}$$ where $\Sigma=\{a,b\}$. What shape do words $w \in L$ have?
> > [!tip]-
> > - $w$ has **odd length**;
> > - $w$ has **even length** such that $w = uv$ with $|u| = |v|$ and $$u(m)\neq v(m)$$ for some $0\leq m< |u|$. Let $n=|u|-m-1$. Then $w$ is in 
> > $$\begin{align}
> > \underbrace{\Sigma^{m}\{a\}\Sigma^{n}}_{u} \; \underbrace{\Sigma^{m}\{b\}\Sigma^{n}}_{v} &\cup \Sigma^{m}\{b\}\Sigma^{n} \; \Sigma^{m}\{a\}\Sigma^{n} \\
> > &= \\
> > \underbrace{\Sigma^{m}\{a\}\Sigma^{m}}_{u} \; \underbrace{\Sigma^{n}\{b\}\Sigma^{n}}_{v} &\cup \Sigma^{m}\{b\}\Sigma^{m} \; \Sigma^{n}\{a\}\Sigma^{n}
> > \end{align}$$
> 
> > [!info]- Solution
> > We give a context-free grammar for the language: 
> > $$\begin{align}
> > S \to AB \,\vert\, BA \,\vert\, A \,\vert\, B \qquad A &\to XAX \,\vert\, a \qquad X \to a \,\vert\, b \\
> > B &\to XBX \,\vert\, b
> > \end{align}$$

# Derivation Trees
Let $G$ be a grammar, and consider a derivation $S\Rightarrow^{*}w$
- If $G$ is right linear, $w$ contains **at most one variable**
- If $G$ is context-free, $w$ can contain **multiple variables**

Two strategies to choose which variable to expand:
- leftmost: always the leftmost variable
- leftmost: always the rightmost variable

A derivation tree for a context-free grammar $G=(V,T,S,P)$:
- Nodes have labels from $V\cup T\cup \{\lambda\}$. The **root** has label $S$.
- A node with label $A\in V$ can have children labelled
	- $x_1,...,x_n$ if there is a rule $A\to x_{1}\cdots x_{n}$ with $n\geq 1$, or
	- $\lambda$ (single child) if there is a rule $A\to \lambda$
- Every node with a label from $T\cup \{\lambda\}$ is a **leaf**

> [!example]-
> In the grammar $S\to SaS \,\vert\, b$, a derivation tree for $bab$ is: 
> ```mermaid
> flowchart TD
> A[S] --> B[S] --> D[b]
> A --> a
> A --> C[S] --> E[b]
> ```

- The labels of the leaves of a derivation tree, read from left to right (skipping $\lambda$), form a word in $L(G)$
# Ambiguity
A context-free grammar $G$ is **ambiguous** if there exists a word $w\in L(G)$ for which there are multiple derivation trees.

> [!question]- Is the following grammar ambiguous? $S\to S+S \,\vert\, 0$
> Yes since there are two derivation trees for $0+0+0$

- Ambiguity is typically unwanted:
	- derivation trees often used to assign meaning to words
	- multiple derivation trees may result in double meaning

> [!important]- Ambiguity is undecidable
> That is, there exists no algorithm that decides whether a context-free grammar is ambiguous.

- A language $L$ is inherently ambiguous if
	- $L$ is context-free, and
	- every grammar $G$ with $L(G)=L$ is ambiguous

> [!example]- The following context-free language is inherently amgiguous
> $$\{a^{n}b^{m}c^{l} \, \vert\, n=m \lor m=l\}$$
> It is generated by the following ambiguous grammar
> $$\begin{align} S\to A \,\vert\, B \qquad &A \to Ac \,\vert\, C  &&B \to aB \,\vert\, D \\ &C \to aCb \,\vert\, \lambda &&D\to bDc \,\vert\, \lambda\end{align}$$

# Parsing
- Parsing is the search for a derivation tree for a given word

> [!info] THEOREM
> For a context-free grammar, parsing is possible in $O(|w|^{3})$ time. ($w$: length of input)

- For every context-free grammar $G$:
- there exists an algorithm and $C\geq 0$ such that
- for every word $w$, the algorithm determines in at most $$C\cdot |w|^{3} \text{ steps}$$ whether $w\in L(G)$ and a derivation tree if $w\in L(G)$
