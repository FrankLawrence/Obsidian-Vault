# Regular Grammars
- a **grammar** defines a language
	- with **grammar rules** we can construct a $\langle$sentence$\rangle$

> [!definition] Grammar
> A **grammar** $G=(V,T,S,P)$ consists of:
> - finite set $V$ of **non-terminals** (or **variables**)
> - finite set $T$ of **terminals**
> - a **start symbol** $S\in V$
> - finite set $P$ of **production rules** $x\to y$ where
> 	- $x \in (V\cup T)^{+}$ containing at least one symbol from $V$
> 	- $y \in (V\cup T)^{*}$

- A grammar is **context-free** if $x \in V$ for every rule $x\to y$
- The BNF (Backus Naur Form) is often used to define the syntax of programming languages. These are context-free grammars!
	- non-terminals are indicated by $\langle$ and $\rangle$
- If $x \to y$ is a production rule, then we have a **derivation step** $$uxv \Rightarrow uyv$$ for every $u,v \in (V\cup T)^{*}$

> [!example]
> $G=(\{S\}, \{a,b\}, S, P)$, where $P$ consists of $$S\to aSb \qquad S \to \lambda$$
> Example derivations: 
> $$\begin{align*}
> S &\Rightarrow \lambda &&S \Rightarrow^{*}\lambda\\
> S &\Rightarrow aSb \Rightarrow ab &&S \Rightarrow^{*} ab\\
> S &\Rightarrow aSb \Rightarrow aaSbb \Rightarrow aabb &&S \Rightarrow^{*} aabb\\
>\end{align*}$$

- A **derivation** $\Rightarrow^{*}$ is the reflexive, transitive closure of $\Rightarrow$
- The [[Finite Automata#Regular Languages|language]] generated by a grammar $G=(V,T,S,P)$ is $$L(G)=\{w\in T^{*}\,|\, S\Rightarrow^{*}w\}$$
	- the words contain only terminal symbols
	- the words can be derived from the start symbol
- **upper case** letters are variables
- **lower case** letters are terminals
- $V\to w_{1}\,|\,\cdots\,|\, w_{n}$ is shorthand for $n$ rules $$\begin{array}{c} V \to w_{1} \\ \vdots \\ V \to w_{n}\end{array}$$

> [!question]- Find a grammar $G$ such that $L(G)=\{a,b\}^{*}\{c\}\{b,c\}^{*}$
> $$\begin{align*}
> S &\to XcY \\
> X &\to aX \,|\, bX \,|\, \lambda\\
> Y &\to bY \,|\, cY \,|\, \lambda\\
> \end{align*}$$

# Right linear Grammars and Regular Languages
- A grammar $G=(V,T,S,P)$ is **right linear** if all production rules are of the form $$A \to uB \quad \text{or} \quad A\to u$$ with $A,B \in V$ and $u\in T^{*}$. Moreover $G$ is **strictly right linear** if $|u|\leq 1$ (i.e. $u\in (T\cup \{\lambda\}$)
- Let $G$ be a right linear grammar. There exists a strictly right linear grammar $H$ such that $L(G)=L(H)$
- For every NFA $M$ there exists a right linear grammar $G$ with $L(G)=L(M)$
- For every right linear grammar $G$ there exists an NFA $M$ with $L(M)=L(G)$

> [!info]+ Construction strictly right linear grammar from grammar
> Let $G=(V,T,S,P)$ be a right linear grammar. Assume that we have a production rule $\gamma$ of the form $$A \to u(B)$$ with $|u|> 1$. Then $u=aw$ for some $a\in T$ and $w\in T^{+}$. Let $X$ be a *fresh* variable; $X\notin (V\cup T)$. We add $X$ to $V$ and split the rule $\gamma$ into: $$A\to aX \qquad X \to w(B)$$
> Then $a\to aX\to aw(B)=u(B)$. It follows $L(G)=L(H)$. Repeat splitting until $|u| \leq 1$ for all rules.

> [!info]+ Construction strictly right linear grammar from NFA
> Let $M=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,\{q_{0}\},F)$ be an NFA with a single starting state. Define $G=(V,T,S,P)$ with $V=Q$ and $T=\Sigma$ and $S=q_{0}$. The set $P$ consists of the following production rules 
> $$\begin{align}q &\to \alpha q' \quad &&\text{for every } q' \in \delta(q,\alpha) \text{ where } \alpha \in \Sigma\cup \{\lambda\} \\ q &\to \lambda &&\text{for every }q \in F\end{align}$$
> Then $$A\Rightarrow^{*} uB \text{ in } G \Longleftrightarrow A \overset{u}{\twoheadrightarrow}B \text{ in } M$$

> [!info]+ Construction NFA from strictly right linear grammar
> Let $G=(V,T,S,P)$ be a right linear grammar. All rules are of form $$A\to u \quad \text{or} \quad A\to uB$$ for $A,B\in V, u\in (T\cup \{\lambda\})$. Let NFA $M=(Q, \Sigma,\delta,\{S\},F)$ with $$\Sigma=T\qquad Q= V\cup \{\Omega\} \qquad F=\{\Omega\}$$ where $\Omega\notin V$. The transitions $\delta$ are given by 
> $$\begin{align*}
> A &\overset{u}{\to}B \quad && \text{for every $A\to uB$ in $G$}\\
> A &\overset{u}{\to}\Omega && \text{for every $A\to u$ in $G$}\\
> \end{align*}$$
> Then $S\Rightarrow^{*}w\in T^{*} \Longleftrightarrow M \text{ accepts } w$.

- Language $L$ is regular $\Longleftrightarrow$ there is a right and left linear grammar $G$ with $L(G)=L$

# Left Linear Grammars
- A grammar $G=(V,T,S,P)$ is **left linear** if all production rules are of the form $$A \to Bu \quad \text{or} \quad A\to u$$ with $A,B \in V$ and $u\in T^{*}$.

> [!attention] Mixing right and left linear rules, the generated language is not always regular. 
> Let $G$ be the grammar
> $$\begin{align*}
> S &\to aA\\
> A &\to Sb\\
> S &\to \lambda
> \end{align*}$$ 
> Every rule of $G$ is either right or left linear, however the language $L(G)=\{a^{n}b^{n}\,|\, n\geq 0\}$ is not regular
